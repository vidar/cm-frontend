# Outreach drafts

Four short pieces about chessmoments.com, revised after two rounds of critique and checked against the code: an email for a club or federation secretary (paste into your mail client, fill in the bracketed parts, and attach or link a real event page of the same kind as theirs); a forum post (the title goes in the thread title, the rest in the body; replace both `[link]` placeholders with pages you have read yourself before posting); a reply you can paste when someone in that thread asks how the site differs from chess-results.com or Lichess broadcasts; and a blurb for organisers to paste wherever the upload feature is mentioned (the homepage, a federation newsletter, a message to an organiser). Nothing in them promises more than the site does today; the "Not applied" notes at the end say which suggested changes were left out and why.

## 2. Forum post

**Title:** chessmoments.com: TWIC tournaments as pages with standings and round reports; any game can be analysed by Stockfish and annotated by AI (hobby project)

chessmoments.com is a hobby project. It holds about 3.5 million over-the-board games from The Week in Chess (2012 onwards, with Mark Crowther's permission). For every tournament the site works out the format and builds the pages by itself: a crosstable for a round robin, standings for a Swiss, a bracket for a knockout, team standings for a league, and a page per round (an event whose Round tags can't be used gets a plain list of games). Where TWIC has only the top boards (most big opens) it shows the scores in those games and says so; they are not the official standings. Every game can be replayed.

On any game page, "Analyse with Stockfish" (Stockfish 17.1, depth 18) gives you an eval graph with inaccuracies, mistakes and blunders marked. After that, "Write notes with AI" produces a short story of the game and notes under the key moves, plus a paragraph on where the players stood in the tournament before the game and another on what the result changed. The notes are written in plain language for players around 1200–1900: they say White is slightly better rather than quoting centipawns, and may only use moves that are in the game or in the engine's lines. Here is one to look at: [link to a game whose analysis and notes you have read]. On a match game the paragraph before the game gives the match score going into it, and the AI is asked to write a note on every move the engine marks as a mistake or blunder, with the line it preferred.

Limits: the AI only sees the engine output and a text summary of the standings, not the board, so it can misread why a move is bad or muddle the tournament story; notes are written once and shown to everyone, so please report bad ones. There is a daily cap on new notes. Engine analysis takes about a minute a game and is saved for everyone; there is one engine box, so when its queue is long you are asked to come back later. Nothing is live: I load new TWIC issues by hand, so a tournament shows up days or weeks after it ends, and coverage starts in 2012. No bulk download or API: TWIC's permission covers showing the games, so you can copy the PGN of a game from its page but not the database; for the files go to TWIC. One person runs it in spare time on a hobby budget, so it may be slow or down now and then.

Organisers can also upload their own tournament as PGN, round by round, through a secret link. I check each new tournament by hand before it appears; after that, new rounds go live as soon as they are uploaded.

Free and non-commercial, no accounts, no ads. Please try it and tell me what breaks.

## 3. Reply to "How is this different from chess-results.com or Lichess broadcasts?"

It does not replace either. chess-results.com is the official record: pairings, standings, tie-breaks as your regulations define them, and often the games. This site does no pairings and its standings are worked out from the games, so a bye or forfeit that isn't in the PGN doesn't count. Lichess broadcasts do most of this already: live games, replay, standings and computer analysis. What is different here: every event in TWIC since 2012 already has its pages, with nothing to set up; each game page has Stockfish analysis with the mistakes marked and AI notes that include a short paragraph on where the players stood in the event before the game and another on what the result changed; and each player's page collects their games across events, with the other games between the two players shown under each game. For a club event it means the standings, the round pages and every game in one place from a plain PGN file uploaded after each round, with no account for anyone to keep. For TWIC events it is also slow: I load the weekly issues by hand, so a tournament appears days or weeks after it ends; uploaded tournaments appear when the organiser uploads a round. It is one person's hobby project, so keep chess-results or Lichess as the official record and your own PGN files; treat this as the place for the analysis and the notes.

## Not applied

- **"A pairing program like Swiss-Manager doesn't hold the moves" (critique 1, piece 1).** Not stated as a fact: the site's own upload page lists Swiss-Manager and Vega as sources, and pairing programs can export PGN when the moves have been entered. The email says instead that the games are needed as PGN with the moves and that a pairings list alone is not enough, which is the point that matters.
- **"Usually the same day" as the approval turnaround (critique 1, piece 1).** Left as a placeholder; only the site owner knows what turnaround they can keep.
- **"A week or more after it ends" (critique 2, pieces 2 and 3).** The code shows the TWIC import is run by hand with no feed, but not how long it typically lags; the drafts say "days or weeks after it ends".
- **"Every move the engine marks as a mistake gets a note with the line it preferred" (critique 2, piece 2).** Softened to "the AI is asked to write a note on every move the engine marks as a mistake or blunder": that is what the prompt instructs, but the output is not guaranteed and the notes are not checked.
- **Both critiques' example links.** Kept as `[link]` placeholders rather than invented; the forum post and the email should not go out until the owner has replaced them with pages they have read.
- **Piece 1 length.** Critique 1's assembled version plus critique 2's additions (rights, link secrecy, limits, no permanence promise) come to about 520 words. Everything in it answers a question a secretary would ask, so it was kept; the owner can cut the limits sentence and the FIDE-ID sentence for a shorter version.
